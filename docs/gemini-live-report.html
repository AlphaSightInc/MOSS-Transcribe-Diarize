Verdict

A Gemini-powered live engine beats self-hosted MOSS on speaker accuracy and passes every hard bar, with the same UI and API. Speaker error before Stop falls from 0.145 to 0.099, the final transcript matches MOSS (0.105 vs 0.110), and the E1 meeting shows 4–5 speaker labels for 4 real voices instead of 10.

The price: speaker labels appear about 11–19 s after speech instead of ~2.5 s, meeting audio goes to Google, and it costs roughly $1.40–3.40 per meeting-hour. It is not "Gemini 3.8 Live" alone: 3.8 Live cannot label speakers, so the winning design combines two other Gemini models with the existing voice-fingerprint model.

Two different delays

"Words visible" and "speaker label visible" measure two different moments for the same spoken word.

WORDS ON SCREEN · 0.7 s

How long after someone says a word it appears anywhere on screen, in the grey Speaker TBD card at the bottom. This is the responsiveness you feel while someone talks: median 0.7 s, 90% within 1.0 s (Gemini 3.5 Transcribe Live, timed against word timestamps on 11 clips). MOSS took about 2.3 s.

SPEAKER NAME · 11–19 s

How long until those same words move into a row with a speaker label (S01, S02 …). It needs the next three-minute "who spoke" window to come back. Nothing is hidden meanwhile: the words are already on screen, only the name is pending. MOSS named speakers in ~2.5 s but often named them wrong.

How it works

A live transcriber has two jobs: words (what was said) and who (who said it). MOSS did both every 2.5 seconds on a tiny slice of audio. A 2.5-second slice is too little evidence to recognise a voice, so the same person kept getting new labels and echo got its own "speaker". The Gemini design splits the jobs and gives the "who" job far more context.

WHY LABELS ARE STABLE

Each "who spoke" call sees up to three minutes of the meeting, so Gemini groups a voice using many sentences, and consecutive windows share 2¾ minutes of audio. Matching speakers across windows is then easy. The voice fingerprint only matters when someone returns after more than three minutes of silence.

WHY LABELS ARE LATE

A row is labelled only after the next window covering it returns: on average half a 15-second tick plus a 5–10 second Gemini call, 11–19 s in total. Until then the words show immediately in the grey preview line. That delay is the trade: slower labels, far fewer wrong ones.

What we found

  • F1Gemini 3.8 Live cannot label speakers. It only answers in speech, and its transcript has no speaker field. As a word source it was also slower (4.2 s median) than Gemini 3.5 Transcribe Live (0.69 s), which won the words job with the lowest word error and zero invented words on silence, noise or music.
  • F2Gemini 3.5 Transcribe labels speakers well inside one call, badly across calls. Labels are local to each call, 30-second windows produced 7 labels for 3 voices, and very long calls return some word timestamps that jump backwards. Growing three-minute windows fixed the seams (E1 4/4, 43-min meeting 5/5).
  • F3Pure Gemini is not enough. On identical calls, the pure-Gemini ledger scored 0.254 settled DER; adding the existing WeSpeaker fingerprint model as a linker scored 0.099. Fingerprints only link voices Gemini already separated; Gemini still decides who spoke each word.
  • F4Mixing the mic into the meeting audio loses you. In the mono mix, 2 of 3 things the local user said over others vanished. Keeping the mic lane separate recovered them; an echo gate keeps speaker echo out (0/20 stray words on E1 with raw −25 dB echo; breaks at echo louder than −15 dB).
  • F5The final pass needs its own identity rule. One whole-recording Gemini call scored 0.125, above the 0.110 bar, because one host's intro was a second voice. Merging labels with fingerprint similarity ≥ 0.65, unless the two labels talk back and forth, reached 0.105 in 4 of 4 repeated draws. Calls are capped at 15 minutes; 30-minute calls merged two different guests.
  • F6End-to-end measurement found 17 product defects that offline numbers hid. Examples: one row per word, the tail left unlabelled at Stop, the preview never shown, the product using different fingerprint audio than the prototype (0.119 vs 0.099), label lag climbing to 30 s on long meetings, and an echo-duplicated preview that also repeated committed text, and a first fix for it that deleted fresh words. All are fixed at the final commit (label lag back to 18–19 s; preview repeats of committed text fell from 64% to 10% of screens without losing new words).
  • F7The screen just before labels arrive is the weak spot. Scored at the moment audio ends, Gemini's labelled surface lags MOSS (0.29 vs 0.20 DER) because the newest ~15 s is still grey preview. It catches up within one window; the settled and final surfaces are better than MOSS.

Your questions

Q1
Gemini 3.8 Live vs Gemini 3.5 Transcribe Live: is 3.5 Transcribe Live enough?

Yes. 3.5 Transcribe Live is the right tool for words, and the final design does not use 3.8 Live at all.

Neither live model labels speakers. That job is done by the batch model, Gemini 3.5 Transcribe, on the three-minute windows.

Q2
Could one pass give both speed and accuracy, instead of a fast pass plus an accurate pass?

No, and the measurements say why. Fast words need a few seconds of audio. Stable speaker names need minutes of it. One Gemini call cannot be both small and large.

  • O1Live stream only. Words in 0.7 s, but it never names speakers. That fails the reason for this project.
  • O2Batch windows only. Short 8-second windows every 2 s gave words in 2.3–3.1 s with worse word error (0.16–0.18) and unstable speakers (30-second windows already split 3 voices into 7). Three-minute windows every 2 s would cost about $16 per hour and still take 5–10 s per call.
  • O3Fast pass + accurate pass (current). The fast pass is display-only: it is never saved and cannot corrupt the transcript, so its failures are cosmetic. Everything saved comes from one authoritative pass. The only coupling is the hand-over from grey card to named row, which is where the duplicate-text bugs lived (all fixed).

Recommendation: keep two passes. The real simplification available is the after-Stop pass (D14): the live pass already scores 0.099 before Stop, against 0.105 after Stop on the six clips. Dropping it would make Stop instant and delete the 15-minute chunking code. The cost is a slightly worse final transcript on long meetings (43-min DER 0.050 instead of 0.034; word error 0.113 instead of 0.104).

Market check: OpenAI and live-diarization services

No major "live" model from Google or OpenAI labels speakers while streaming. OpenAI is not a better fit: its live transcriber costs about 3× more and has no speaker labels, and its only speaker-labelling model is batch-only and shuts down on 26 Feb 2027. Services that do label speakers live exist (Soniox, AssemblyAI, Deepgram, Speechmatics). They could collapse our two passes into one stream, but nobody, including practitioners, calls live speaker labelling solved.

  • C1Our architecture matches the practitioner consensus: one channel per participant (mic = you, meeting audio = others) plus echo removal. One team reported 477 of 641 "me" rows in a 42-minute meeting were echo, and fixed it the way we did (drop mic text contained in nearby meeting text).
  • C2Two reported Gemini failures we had not hit are now guarded. (1) A live stream that stays open but stops sending text is restarted when the batch pass finds ≥ 10 s of speech the stream missed. (2) An after-Stop chunk that comes back with speech missing is retried once, and otherwise keeps the live rows, so the saved transcript never loses what the user saw. A first version used the speech detector (WebRTC) as its witness; it treats music as speech, fired 22 times on one normal meeting and tripled label delay, so it was redesigned. The shipped guards fired 0 times on the same meeting, with delay and cost unchanged.
  • C3One field test of Gemini's batch speaker labels failed (67–75% vs 89% for the incumbent, one person split into up to 9 IDs per window). That is the failure our voice-fingerprint linker exists to repair. It is a warning that our numbers come from our corpora, not everyone's.
  • C4Gemini 3.8 Live's built-in transcript is reported as unreliable (mishears, invents text on silence, arrives only after the utterance), consistent with our decision not to use it.

Risks and limits

  • R1Crosstalk. The four-person RTFL discussion stays hard (settled DER ≈ 0.28–0.52 per pass, MOSS 0.36); its reference is only two-thirds timed.
  • R2Gemini varies run to run and its preview models, prices and quotas can change. Live words report no usage, so their cost is a list-price estimate.
  • R3Loud speaker echo (above −15 dB, without Chrome echo cancellation) and very quiet talkers defeat the mic echo gate. Headphones mode is clean.
  • R4Several people on one laptop mic are shown as one local speaker.
  • R5Long meetings were measured up to 43 minutes. The after-Stop pass takes about a minute per 10–15 minutes of audio; the meeting shows "refining" meanwhile.
  • R6Synthetic spliced meetings (each utterance from a different recording) still over-count speakers; real meetings did not.
  • R8Reported Gemini failures (silent live-stream stall with two sessions; batch calls silently missing content) are guarded (C2). The guards stay quiet on normal audio, but they have not yet seen a real occurrence; the live view can still miss a window until Stop repairs it.
  • R7Not yet exercised: a live attended session with a real microphone and a real browser tab, which you reserved for yourself (D7).
